# Workshop questions

**54 questions covering all 205 abilities in the register.** Each names the abilities it surfaces, who has to
be in the room to answer it, what to listen for, and how to handle a thin answer.

**Nobody runs all 54.** A session uses eight or ten, chosen by which domains are live for this client.
`ASK-01`, `ASK-12`, `ASK-32` and `ASK-48` between them reach 24 abilities across nine domains — a serviceable
first session on their own.

Five need only one person: `ASK-09` · `ASK-11` · `ASK-23` · `ASK-37` · `ASK-41`. Useful when a full workshop
cannot be arranged.

The abilities themselves are in `px-ability-register.md`.

### The rule these are written to

**Ask for an instance, not an assessment.** When did this last happen, who did it, what did it cost. A
question phrased as a judgement gets you the answer the room has been trained to give: *"do you have a single
source of truth?"* is answered yes by everyone including the people who do not. And avoid the ability's own
vocabulary — naming the concept invites the rehearsed reply.

A question that can only be answered one way is not a question. *"Do you have enough resource?"* gets a no
from everybody and is exactly as useless as a universal yes.

#### `ASK-01` — The launch walkthrough

> **“Take the last product you launched. Start from the first moment anyone here knew it existed, and walk me forward to the day it went live.”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-03` `SOURCE-16` `SOURCE-17` `ORGANISE-02` `ORGANISE-12` `ORGANISE-21` `STATUS-07` `ENRICH-14` `MEASURE-09`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever enriches products · whoever deals with suppliers · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Where the very first scrap of information came from, and who held it. How many pairs of hands it passed through, and how many systems. Where it sat waiting, and for whom. Whether anyone could say what the elapsed time was — if they cannot, MEASURE-09 is missing and every later number they quote you is unarguable.

**Note** — If they describe a process rather than a product, stop them and ask for a specific one by name. Processes are how it is supposed to work; products are how it does.

#### `ASK-02` — The missing-data phone call

> **“Who chased a supplier for missing product information last week? What was missing, and how long did it take to arrive?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-08` `SOURCE-12` `SOURCE-13` `SOURCE-22` `ORGANISE-12` `ORGANISE-22`

**Who to invite** — whoever deals with suppliers · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — Whether anyone owns chasing at all, or it happens when someone notices. Whether the same suppliers come up every time — if they can name the bad ones from memory, they have SOURCE-12 informally and could have it properly. Whether anything is automated, or a person is the automation.

**Note** — If nobody chased anyone last week, ask when they last launched something. Either chasing is genuinely solved, or nothing is moving.

#### `ASK-03` — The spreadsheet's journey

> **“When a supplier sends you a spreadsheet, what happens to it? Open the last one if you still can.”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-01` `SOURCE-02` `SOURCE-11` `SOURCE-21` `SOURCE-25` `COLLECT-08` `COLLECT-09` `QUALITY-11` `QUALITY-13`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns the integrations

**Listen for** — Retyping — how much, and by whom. Whether the original still exists anywhere findable, which is SOURCE-25 and only ever matters during an argument. Whether anything is checked before it lands, or after. What happens when the supplier sends a second version of the same file.

**Note** — "Open the last one" is the load-bearing half of the question. If they cannot find it in under a minute, you have your answer about SOURCE-25 without asking.

#### `ASK-04` — The supplier's view

> **“If I were one of your suppliers, what could I see about my own products in your systems — and what would I have to do to change something?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-10` `SOURCE-19` `SOURCE-23` `SOURCE-24` `ORGANISE-03`

**Who to invite** — whoever deals with suppliers · a supplier, if you can get one

**Listen for** — Whether suppliers can see anything at all. Blindness is the normal answer and it surprises people when you name it. Whether the form they fill in is the same for every product type, which is SOURCE-23 and usually reveals a folder of dozens of templates nobody maintains.

**Note** — Ask the same question about an internal colleague who is not on the product team. The answer is often different, and the gap is SOURCE-19.

#### `ASK-05` — The consequence question

> **“If a supplier simply does not send what you asked for, what happens?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-09` `SOURCE-12` `SOURCE-13` `ORGANISE-19`

**Who to invite** — whoever deals with suppliers · whoever holds the budget

**Listen for** — Whether there is any consequence at all. Usually there is not, and the honest answer is "we do it ourselves" — which tells you SOURCE-09 is absent and that the fallback is being paid for out of somebody's week. Whether anyone in the room could impose a consequence if they wanted to, which is really ORGANISE-19.

**Note** — This is the question where SOURCE-09 reveals nothing on purpose. Leverage cannot be bought, so the answer is not a shopping list — it is how much +intaketeam they are quietly funding instead.

#### `ASK-06` — The department that never sends anything

> **“Which team here knows something about your products that never reaches the product record? What do they know, and why does it not arrive?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-15` `SOURCE-17` `SOURCE-19` `ORGANISE-16` `ORGANISE-02` `MASTER-06`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · somebody from outside the product team

**Listen for** — Named departments — sourcing, engineering, QA, legal, packaging. Whether anyone has ever asked them. Whether there is a standing meeting where these people are in the same room, which is ORGANISE-16 and almost never exists. Whether the reason is unwillingness or simply that nobody gave them a way in.

**Note** — If they say "nobody", ask who decides a product's launch date, its compliance status and its dimensions. At least one of those lives outside the product team.

#### `ASK-07` — The person who left

> **“Has anyone left in the last two years who knew things about your products that were not written down? What happened afterwards?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-16` `SOURCE-20` `ORGANISE-24` `QUALITY-03`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — Whether it hurt, and for how long. Whether anything changed as a result — a handover document, a glossary, a second person trained. Most organisations have this story and have drawn no conclusion from it.

**Note** — A soft version for a room that will not admit fragility: "if your longest-serving product person were off for a month, what would stop?"

#### `ASK-08` — The document pile

> **“What arrives as a PDF or a datasheet that somebody then retypes?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-11` `SOURCE-14` `SOURCE-18` `CONTENT-09`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns the integrations

**Listen for** — Volume and who does it. Whether anyone has tried to automate it and what happened — the interview evidence says this path is talked about far more than it is taken. Whether the person retyping can see the original at the moment they need it, which is SOURCE-14.

**Note** — Worth pairing with the AI facet: this is the classic place a client expects extraction to solve everything, and the classic place it disappoints.

#### `ASK-09` — The writer's desk

> **“When someone sits down to write a product description here, what is actually in front of them?”**

**Covers** `SOURCE-14` `CONTENT-01` `CONTENT-02` `CONTENT-12` `ENRICH-01`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — A blank field, or a template. Whether they can see what good looks like. Whether they start from the supplier's words or their own — and whether that was a decision or an accident, which is CONTENT-12. Whether the source material is on the same screen or in an email somebody has to go and find.

**Note** — Ask to watch someone do it for one product. Five minutes of observation beats twenty of description.

### `ORGANISE` — nine more questions

Written second, and written **in dependency order rather than domain order**: the `SOURCE` set had already
answered 8 of `ORGANISE`'s 26 abilities on its way past — `ORGANISE-02`, `-03`, `-12`, `-16`, `-19`, `-21`,
`-22` and `-24` — so these nine target the remaining 18 and do not ask for the same stories twice.

This is the domain where the money is, and it is the hardest to ask about directly. Nobody answers *"is this
properly funded?"* honestly. Every question below approaches an uncomfortable subject through a concrete
event instead: a change request, a new starter, a mistake that went out, a project that ended.

#### `ASK-10` — The change request

> **“Think of the last time somebody asked for a change to how product information works — a new field, a new rule, an extra step. What happened to that request?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-10` `ORGANISE-11` `ORGANISE-15` `ORGANISE-19` `MODEL-17` `MODEL-18`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever owns the integrations · whoever holds the budget

**Listen for** — Who they had to ask, and how long it took. Whether there is a queue and whether anyone owns it. Whether the answer was "we cannot until the next project" — which is ORGANISE-15 missing, and the single most common finding in this domain. If the change was made by the business without IT, MODEL-17 is present and worth a great deal.

**Note** — This asks about money without mentioning money. People will describe a capacity problem honestly long before they will describe a budget one.

#### `ASK-11` — Whose budget

> **“When product information work needs money — a licence, an integration, another person — whose budget does it come out of?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-11` `ORGANISE-15` `ORGANISE-17` `ORGANISE-18`

**Who to invite** — whoever holds the budget

**Listen for** — Whether there is a line of its own, or it rides on somebody else's. Riding is normal and not a failure — but ask the follow-up: **"and if that team stopped caring about product data, what would happen?"** That is the whole risk of ORGANISE-18 in one sentence, and the room usually goes quiet.

**Note** — If nobody in the room knows the answer, that is itself the finding. Work that nobody can name a budget for is work nobody is defending.

#### `ASK-12` — The new starter

> **“Somebody joins on Monday and will be enriching products. Walk me through their first two weeks.”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-05` `ORGANISE-06` `ORGANISE-07` `ORGANISE-08` `ORGANISE-09` `CONTENT-02` `CONTENT-06`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — How many days before they touch live data, and whether anyone is uncomfortable about that. Whether a safe place to practise exists, or the practice is production. How long the account takes and who creates it by hand. Whether there is training material or a colleague who explains it each time — the second is ORGANISE-08 being done by a person who does not know that is their job.

**Note** — The most productive question in this domain, because everyone can answer it concretely and nobody has rehearsed it.

#### `ASK-13` — The thing that went out wrong

> **“Tell me about a time something wrong went out — wrong price, wrong image, wrong specification. How was it found, how was it fixed, and what changed afterwards?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-14` `ORGANISE-22` `ORGANISE-23` `ORGANISE-26` `QUALITY-06` `QUALITY-16` `MEASURE-07`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · somebody from the contact centre · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Whether they found it or a customer did. How long between the error and anyone knowing. Whether anybody could put it back — ORGANISE-23 and QUALITY-06 are usually assumed present and are often not. And the last clause matters most: **what changed afterwards.** Nothing changing is a finding about the organisation, not about the error.

**Note** — If they cannot think of one, they are protecting somebody. Ask instead what the contact centre complains about.

#### `ASK-14` — The approval

> **“Before something goes live, who has to say yes? And has anyone ever actually said no?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-14` `ORGANISE-19` `ORGANISE-25` `ENRICH-07` `CONTENT-08`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever answers to the regulator

**Listen for** — Whether sign-off is real or ceremonial. **A gate nobody has ever failed is not a gate**, and the second half of the question is the one that finds out. Whether the approval is for content, for technical accuracy, or because the law requires it — three different things that often share one signature. Whether it lives in a system or in somebody's inbox.

**Note** — Where sign-off exists and is enforced by a system, ORGANISE-25 is present at least in part — and that is the one place encoded workflow earns its keep regardless of size.

#### `ASK-15` — How work arrives

> **“How does somebody here know there is product work waiting for them?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-12` `ORGANISE-13` `ORGANISE-21` `ORGANISE-25` `ENRICH-08`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Email, a shared sheet, a dashboard, or a colleague walking over. All four are viable and the last one is not automatically wrong — it is ORGANISE-21 working. What matters is whether anything happens when work goes late, and whether anyone can see the queue as a whole.

**Note** — Pair with ORGANISE-21 versus ORGANISE-25 explicitly. If the walking-over method still works at their size, encoding it would cost more than it returns.

#### `ASK-16` — The other teams

> **“Do the different teams that touch product information do it the same way? Where do they diverge, and does the divergence matter?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-01` `ORGANISE-05` `ORGANISE-24` `QUALITY-04` `QUALITY-08`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · somebody from outside the product team

**Listen for** — Business units with their own conventions, usually invisible from the centre. Whether anyone has tried to standardise, and what happened to them. Whether the same word means different things in two places — that is ORGANISE-24, and it is cheap to fix early and expensive later.

**Note** — "Does it matter" is doing real work here. Divergence that harms nobody is not a finding, and treating it as one is how consultants lose rooms.

#### `ASK-17` — The last change of habit

> **“When did you last change how people actually work with product information — and how did that land?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-01` `ORGANISE-04` `ORGANISE-08` `ORGANISE-20`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — Whether anybody owned the change beyond delivering training. Who resisted, and whether that was ever addressed or just outlasted. Whether it stuck — ask what people do today, not what was rolled out.

**Note** — ORGANISE-20 is the ability most often mistaken for ORGANISE-08. Training tells people what the new thing is; it does nothing about the person who preferred the old one.

#### `ASK-18` — After the project

> **“When your last product information project finished, what happened to the team?”**

**Covers** `ORGANISE-04` `ORGANISE-10` `ORGANISE-11` `ORGANISE-15` `ORGANISE-17`

**Who to invite** — whoever holds the budget · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Dispersed, retained, or partly retained. What degraded in the six months afterwards, and whether anyone noticed at the time. People usually stay; **what leaves is IT time**, and that gap is invisible until the first change request lands.

**Note** — If they have never finished one, ask what they expect to happen. The expectation is as informative as the history, and it is usually optimistic.

### `ASSETS` — eight questions, and the diagnostic left to fire on its own

The first two sets never reached this domain: all 20 abilities were open. Written third because `ASSETS`
carries the model's one proven diagnostic, so its questions do double duty.

**The four DAM triggers are deliberately never asked about directly.** `ASSETS-11`, `-12`, `-14` and `-19`
are spread across `ASK-19`, `ASK-20`, `ASK-23` and `ASK-24` — four separate conversations about a
photograph, the kinds of picture, the designer's copy, and volume. Nobody is asked whether they need a
digital asset management system, because that question invites either an aspiration or a defence. **If three
or four of those questions produce a wince, the client has described a DAM without being led there**, and the
finding is theirs rather than yours.

#### `ASK-19` — One photograph's journey

> **“Take one product photograph. Where was it made, and what happened to it between there and appearing on a page?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-01` `ASSETS-04` `ASSETS-06` `ASSETS-11` `ASSETS-14` `ASSETS-16` `MASTER-03`

**Who to invite** — whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — How many hands it passed through, and whether any of them renamed, resized or cropped it by hand. Where the original sits now and whether that place is a system or a drive. Whether anything was recorded about the image at the moment it arrived, or whether the filename is the only metadata. **This is the first of the DAM triggers — if the honest answer involves a person doing the same three actions on every image, `ASSETS-11` is absent and expensive.**

**Note** — Ask for one photograph, not the process. The process is automated in the telling; a specific image is not.

#### `ASK-20` — The kinds of picture

> **“What different kinds of picture does one product need — and who makes each kind?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-02` `ASSETS-12` `ASSETS-13` `ASSETS-20` `STATUS-01`

**Who to invite** — whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs · a merchandiser · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Whether they distinguish a plain shot from an environment shot from a drawing, or call them all "images". Who commissions each — the second half of the question is where `+studio` surfaces. Whether any kind is routinely missing and the gap is simply tolerated, which is `ASSETS-20`. Whether they can say which image goes in which slot, or the order is whatever was uploaded first.

**Note** — Second DAM trigger. An organisation that names three or four distinct kinds is describing an asset model, whether or not it has one.

#### `ASK-21` — The wrong picture

> **“Has the wrong image ever gone out? How did that happen?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-07` `ASSETS-10` `ASSETS-13` `ASSETS-15` `QUALITY-16`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns a channel · somebody from the contact centre

**Listen for** — Variant confusion above all — the blue one showing the red one's photograph. That is `ASSETS-15`, and it is the single most common asset failure. Whether reuse caused it: one image used in many places, changed once, wrong everywhere else. Whether anybody checks before publication, or the customer is the check.

**Note** — If they say it has never happened, ask how they would know. Wrong images are usually found by customers and reported to somebody who is not in this room.

#### `ASK-22` — Permission

> **“Where did your product images come from — and are you allowed to use all of them?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-05` `ASSETS-18` `SOURCE-24`

**Who to invite** — whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs · whoever answers to the regulator · whoever deals with suppliers

**Listen for** — Supplier-provided, agency-shot, bought from a library, pulled from a data pool. Whether anybody knows the terms attached to each. Whether any right has an expiry date and whether anyone is tracking it. **The usual answer is a pause**, and the pause is the finding.

**Note** — This is the model's one `◐` line, salience genuinely unknown. Ask it of every client for now and record the reaction — that is how the marker gets resolved.

#### `ASK-23` — The designer's copy

> **“When somebody makes a catalogue, a store display or an advertisement, how do they get the images?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-03` `ASSETS-19` `DELIVER-16` `ASSETS-10`

**Who to invite** — whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs

**Listen for** — Whether they ask a person, receive a link, or help themselves. Whether they get the current version or a copy from six months ago that is now wrong. Whether print needs a different file from web and who makes it. **Third DAM trigger, and the one most often missing entirely** — a retailer producing print material with no route from the asset store to the layout tool is doing that work twice.

**Note** — Ask who they would email if that person were away. A single name is `ASSETS-19` being performed by a human.

#### `ASK-24` — Volume, and changing everything

> **“Roughly how many images do you hold — and what happens when you need to change something about all of them?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-04` `ASSETS-08` `ASSETS-09` `ASSETS-14` `ENRICH-05`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations · whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs

**Listen for** — Whether they know the number at all. Whether a bulk change is possible or would be done one at a time. Whether different markets and channels need different files and how that is handled today. Storage limits, and whether anyone has hit one.

**Note** — Fourth DAM trigger. Volume alone rarely justifies a separate system; volume plus per-channel variants nearly always does.

#### `ASK-25` — Finding one again

> **“If I asked for every image of one product taken in the last two years, how would you find them?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-01` `ASSETS-06` `ASSETS-10` `ASSETS-17`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs

**Listen for** — Filename conventions doing the work of metadata. Whether search exists and whether it searches anything but the name. Whether the honest answer is a person's name — "I would ask Sara" — which locates the asset index in somebody's memory.

**Note** — Also surfaces `ASSETS-17`: if nobody describes assets for findability internally, nobody is describing them for search engines either.

#### `ASK-26` — Documents, not pictures

> **“What about documents — manuals, certificates, drawings, safety sheets? Do they travel the same way as images?”**

**Covers** `ASSETS-02` `ASSETS-16` `ASSETS-18` `DELIVER-04` `DELIVER-06` `MASTER-04`

**Who to invite** — whoever answers to the regulator · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Almost always a different and worse answer than the one you just got about images. Which system holds them, and whether it is a document system nobody thinks of as part of product information. Whether versions are kept, which matters for anything a regulator may ask about later.

**Note** — The most reliably neglected question in this domain. Images get attention because they are visible; documents get attention after an audit.

#### `ASK-27` — Every place it ends up

> **“List every place your product information ends up. Include the ones nobody talks about.”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-01` `DELIVER-02` `DELIVER-03` `DELIVER-07` `DELIVER-10` `PRESENT-16`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations · whoever owns a channel · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — The long tail, which is where the finding is: a spreadsheet emailed to a partner every Friday, a store system nobody owns, a finance feed, a printer. Whether the list surprises the people in the room — it usually surprises at least one of them. Whether everything on it comes from one place or several, which is `DELIVER-03`.

**Note** — Ask them to keep going after they stop. The first six destinations are the architecture; the next four are the truth.

#### `ASK-28` — Adding one more

> **“If you signed a new marketplace tomorrow, what would it take to start sending them product data?”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-02` `DELIVER-08` `DELIVER-13` `DELIVER-17` `PRESENT-11`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Weeks or months, and who does the work. Whether the master itself would have to change — if it would, `DELIVER-17` is absent and every new channel is a project. Whether anybody has actually done it recently, or this is hypothetical. Whether the partner's own format and classification standard are treated as a solved problem or a surprise each time.

**Note** — The clearest test of whether an integration platform exists in fact rather than on a diagram.

#### `ASK-29` — The lag

> **“Somebody corrects a product description at ten in the morning. When does the customer see it?”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-03` `DELIVER-18` `COLLECT-05` `PRESENT-10`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Minutes, overnight, or next week. Whether different channels give different answers — they usually do, and the gap between the fastest and slowest is `PRESENT-10` failing in practice. Whether anybody in the room actually knows, or they are guessing.

**Note** — One of the few questions with a numeric answer. Write the number down; it is the most quotable finding in this domain.

#### `ASK-30` — Somebody's spreadsheet

> **“Does anyone here build a file for one particular customer or partner — their products, their prices, their format?”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-07` `DELIVER-09` `DELIVER-10` `DELIVER-11`

**Who to invite** — a merchandiser · whoever owns the integrations

**Listen for** — Who makes it, how often, and how long it takes them. Whether it combines product information with something from another system — pricing, stock, customer terms — which is `DELIVER-11` and usually done by hand in a spreadsheet. Whether they would call this work "product information" at all; often it sits with sales and is invisible to everyone here.

**Note** — This question finds hidden headcount. The answer is frequently one person and two days a month, repeated across several partners.

#### `ASK-31` — The legal minimum

> **“What has to appear on a listing by law — and who makes sure it is there?”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-05` `DELIVER-12` `DELIVER-14` `MASTER-04` `STATUS-09`

**Who to invite** — whoever answers to the regulator · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Safety information, responsible-person details, energy labels, passports, restricted markets. Whether anybody owns this or it is assumed to be somebody else's job. Whether it is checked before publication or discovered afterwards. **A confident, specific answer here is rare and is itself a strong signal about `+compliance`.**

**Note** — If the room looks at each other, that is the finding. Regulatory obligations on product listings have grown faster than most organisations' ownership of them.

#### `ASK-32` — Two product pages

> **“Show me a product page you are proud of — and one you are not.”**

**Covers** `PRESENT-04` `PRESENT-05` `PRESENT-07` `PRESENT-09` `PRESENT-12` `CONTENT-11`

**Who to invite** — a merchandiser · whoever owns a channel · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — What actually differs. Whether the bad one is bad because the data is thin or because the template cannot hold what it needs — those lead to completely different work. Whether they can change a layout at all without a development project. Whether the good one is good because somebody spent a day on it by hand, which does not scale.

**Note** — The single most efficient question in the register. Two minutes of looking replaces twenty of description, and clients enjoy answering it.

#### `ASK-33` — Finding it without knowing the name

> **“How does a customer find a product on your site if they do not know what it is called?”**

**Covers** `PRESENT-01` `PRESENT-02` `PRESENT-03` `PRESENT-05` `PRESENT-06` `MODEL-13`

**Who to invite** — a merchandiser · whoever owns a channel · whoever reports the numbers

**Listen for** — Filters, categories, search, or luck. Who decides what is filterable — and whether the attributes behind the filters are governed or were added by whoever built the page. Whether category structure and filter structure are the same thing or two things maintained separately.

**Note** — Follow with: "and how do you know whether that is working?" It bridges cleanly into `MEASURE`.

#### `ASK-34` — Ask the assistant

> **“Has anybody here asked an AI assistant about one of your products? What did it say?”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-15` `PRESENT-03` `PRESENT-18` `QUALITY-15`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns a channel · a merchandiser

**Listen for** — Whether they have tried at all. Whether the answer was right, and where it came from — often a competitor's page or a marketplace listing rather than their own. Whether anyone owns this, which is nearly always no.

**Note** — **Do it live in the room.** It takes ninety seconds and produces a reaction no amount of description will. The two abilities here are the model's newest and least settled — real reactions are how they get evidence.

#### `ASK-35` — Everywhere else

> **“Where else does a customer meet this product — a shop, a catalogue, a package, a partner's site? Is it the same information?”**

**Covers** `DELIVER-09` `PRESENT-08` `PRESENT-10` `PRESENT-11` `PRESENT-16` `PRESENT-17`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns a channel · somebody who works in a shop · whoever makes the catalogues and the photographs

**Listen for** — Divergence, and whether anyone is troubled by it. Who maintains each surface and whether they talk. Whether packaging and label text comes from the same place as everything else — it almost never does, and it is the one surface where being wrong is expensive to fix.

**Note** — `PRESENT-10` is the ability clients name themselves, so this is where they will say it. Let them; a client's own words are worth more in a report than yours.

### The remaining domains — twelve questions

`COLLECT`, `MIGRATE`, `MASTER`, `STATUS`, `CONTEXT`, `ENRICH`, `CONTENT`, `QUALITY` and `MEASURE`, all of
which had been partly answered in passing by the earlier sets. These twelve close the remainder.

#### `ASK-36` — Where you look

> **“When you need to know something true about a product, where do you look? And is that where everybody else looks?”**

**Covers** `MASTER-01` `MASTER-02` `COLLECT-01` `COLLECT-07`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns product information · somebody from outside the product team

**Listen for** — Whether one place is named, or several with a pause first. The second half matters more than the first — a master that only the product team treats as the master is not one. Which systems people go to instead, and why they trust those.

**Note** — Ask two people separately if you can. Divergent answers to this question are the cheapest evidence you will get all day.

#### `ASK-37` — The feeds

> **“What sends product data into your systems automatically — and what happens when one of those stops?”**

**Covers** `COLLECT-01` `COLLECT-02` `COLLECT-03` `COLLECT-04` `COLLECT-06` `COLLECT-10`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations

**Listen for** — Whether anybody would notice a silent failure, and how. Whether each feed was built its own way by whoever was available — that is `COLLECT-06` absent, and it compounds every year. Whether incoming products are matched on their identifier or on something more hopeful.

**Note** — "What happens when one stops" is the useful half. Everyone can describe the happy path.

#### `ASK-38` — The last move

> **“When did you last move product information from one system into another? What did that actually cost?”**

**Covers** `MIGRATE-01` `MIGRATE-02` `MIGRATE-03` `MIGRATE-04`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Whether they cleaned before moving or moved and then cleaned. How much was reshaped by hand. Whether anyone would do it the same way again. If the answer is "we are doing it now", the whole domain is live rather than historical and worth more time.

**Note** — Every organisation does this once a system generation, so an honest answer here predicts the cost of the next one — which is often the conversation they actually want to have.

#### `ASK-39` — Markets and languages

> **“How many markets and languages do you publish in — and what genuinely differs between them?”**

**Covers** `CONTEXT-01` `CONTEXT-02` `CONTEXT-03` `CONTEXT-04` `CONTEXT-05` `MASTER-05` `ENRICH-11`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Whether "differs" means translated, or genuinely different products, prices and assortments. Whether anyone decided what is allowed to vary, or it varies wherever somebody once needed it to. Where translation happens and whether it is one place or many.

**Note** — Ask what is the same rather than what differs if the room stalls. People find the shared baseline easier to describe.

#### `ASK-40` — Stopping and starting

> **“How do you stop selling something? And how do you start?”**

**Covers** `STATUS-02` `STATUS-03` `STATUS-04` `STATUS-05` `STATUS-06` `STATUS-08`

**Who to invite** — a merchandiser · whoever owns product information · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — How many separate flags are involved, and whether anyone can list them. Whether "visible", "sellable" and "in stock" are distinguishable or one switch. Whether a launch can be scheduled or somebody has to be at a keyboard at midnight — that is `STATUS-08`, and the answer is often a person's alarm clock.

**Note** — The launch half usually gets a better answer than the stopping half. Discontinuation is where the mess lives.

#### `ASK-41` — One product, enriched

> **“Sit with me through one product being enriched. What is slow, and what is repetitive?”**

**Covers** `ENRICH-02` `ENRICH-03` `ENRICH-04` `ENRICH-10` `ENRICH-13` `CONTENT-05` `CONTENT-07`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — Where they switch windows, and how often. What gets typed that could have been calculated. Whether they can work on a set of products or only one at a time. Whether the tool fits how they actually work, including the copy-and-paste nobody admits to.

**Note** — Watch rather than ask, if they will let you. Ten minutes of observation beats an hour of description, and people under-report their own workarounds.

#### `ASK-42` — What the machine does

> **“What here is done by a machine rather than a person — and how do you find out when it got something wrong?”**

**Covers** `ENRICH-03` `ENRICH-06` `ENRICH-09` `ENRICH-11` `ENRICH-12` `QUALITY-10` `QUALITY-14`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns the integrations

**Listen for** — Rules, translation, generated text, data pulled from outside. Whether anybody can tell afterwards which values a machine produced — that is `QUALITY-14`, and it becomes urgent the moment generated content is at any scale. Whether wrong output is caught by review or by a customer.

**Note** — This is the AI facet's question. Expect more enthusiasm than practice, and note the gap rather than correcting it.

#### `ASK-43` — Ready

> **“What makes a product ready to go live? Who decides — and can you see how many are ready right now?”**

**Covers** `QUALITY-01` `QUALITY-02` `QUALITY-05`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Whether "ready" is written down or held as judgement. Whether it differs by channel and by product type, which is `QUALITY-02` and is nearly always needed and rarely built. Whether the number is visible on a screen or requires somebody to go and count.

**Note** — The third clause is the test. An organisation that cannot see readiness cannot manage it, whatever the definition says.

#### `ASK-44` — Locked, and doubled

> **“Is there information nobody is allowed to change? And has the same product ever ended up in your system twice?”**

**Covers** `QUALITY-07` `QUALITY-12`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever owns the integrations

**Listen for** — What is protected and why — usually identifiers and prices, occasionally nothing. On duplicates: whether it has happened, whether anybody would notice, and whether the two records were ever reconciled or just left. **Duplicates are the question people are least prepared for**, because nobody writes a requirement for them until after the first one.

**Note** — Both halves point at `+mdm`. Two yeses here and product information alone will not fix what they are describing.

#### `ASK-45` — Whose words are these

> **“Read me a product description off your site. Who wrote it — you, the supplier, or a machine? And would a competitor's page say the same thing?”**

**Covers** `CONTENT-03` `CONTENT-04` `CONTENT-10` `QUALITY-09`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · a merchandiser

**Listen for** — Whether anyone knows the answer to the first question. Whether the text explains anything to a buyer or lists specifications at them, which is `CONTENT-10`. And the last clause: if it is supplier copy, the honest answer is yes — and whether that troubles them tells you whether `QUALITY-09` is a live concern or a new idea.

**Note** — Have the competitor's page open. This is the one place where showing beats asking, and it takes fifteen seconds.

#### `ASK-46` — What gets measured

> **“What do you measure about product information today — and who looks at it?”**

**Covers** `MEASURE-01` `MEASURE-02` `MEASURE-05` `MEASURE-06`

**Who to invite** — whoever reports the numbers · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Whether anything is measured beyond completeness. Whether customer signals — reviews, returns, search behaviour, support contacts — reach the people who write the content. Who the audience is: if the only reader is the product team, it is not yet reporting on the business.

**Note** — "Who looks at it" is where most measurement conversations end honestly. Numbers nobody reads are a cost, not a capability.

#### `ASK-47` — Where the effort goes

> **“How do you decide which products get attention? And how would you argue for more budget next year?”**

**Covers** `MEASURE-03` `MEASURE-04` `MEASURE-08` `ORGANISE-17`

**Who to invite** — whoever holds the budget · whoever owns product information · whoever reports the numbers

**Listen for** — Whether prioritisation is deliberate — worst information, highest returns, best sellers, live campaigns — or whoever shouts. What they would put in a business case, and whether they know it will be proxies. Whether anything learned about products has ever reached the people who develop or price them.

**Note** — Nobody has ever proved what a product information change earned. If they claim they can, ask to see it — gently, because the claim is usually inherited rather than invented.

### `MODEL` — seven questions, and a different technique

Left until last because it is the most structural domain in the register and none of the previous 47
questions had reached it. **No story reveals whether an organisation supports four levels of variant** — so
these do not ask about structure at all. They ask about *products*: the awkward one, the one with versions,
the one built to order. **Real products are what break data models**, and describing one surfaces the
structure without anybody needing the vocabulary for it.

#### `ASK-48` — The awkward product

> **“Which of your products is hardest to fit into your system? Tell me about that one.”**

**Covers** `MODEL-02` `MODEL-03` `MODEL-04` `MODEL-06` `MODEL-15` `MODEL-20`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · whoever owns product information · a merchandiser

**Listen for** — **Everything.** This is the question that makes a structural domain answerable, because a real product is a story and a data model is not. Whatever they name — the configurable one, the bundle, the service, the one-off second-hand unit, the thing with four levels of variant — is where the model already fails. Listen for the workaround they describe as normal.

**Note** — The single most valuable question in the register for `MODEL`, and it works because nobody has to understand data modelling to answer it. Ask for the second-hardest too.

#### `ASK-49` — One product, several versions

> **“Take something that comes in several versions — sizes, colours, capacities. How is that held, and how does a customer see it?”**

**Covers** `MODEL-04` `MODEL-08` `MODEL-12`

**Who to invite** — whoever enriches products · a merchandiser · whoever owns a channel

**Listen for** — Whether versions are one record or many, and how deep the nesting goes before it breaks. What is shared between them and what is not — which is `MODEL-12`, and the answer is usually discovered rather than designed. Whether the customer sees one product with choices or several products that happen to look alike.

**Note** — Where the internal answer and the customer-facing answer differ, `MODEL-08` is being done by hand somewhere downstream.

#### `ASK-50` — What connects to what

> **“Which products relate to other products — accessories, spare parts, replacements, things sold together? Who decides those links, and do they point both ways?”**

**Covers** `MODEL-06` `MODEL-07` `MODEL-14` `MODEL-16`

**Who to invite** — a merchandiser · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — Who owns the relationships: merchandising, the supplier, or an algorithm. Whether a link made in one direction appears in the other — `MODEL-14`, and the answer is often "I assume so". Whether any relations appear automatically from classification, or every one is made by hand.

**Note** — Ask how many relations exist. If nobody can estimate, they are unmanaged rather than absent.

#### `ASK-51` — The shared things

> **“What information is shared across many products — brands, materials, certifications, colours? Where does that live?”**

**Covers** `MODEL-05` `MODEL-09` `MODEL-15` `MODEL-16`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · whoever enriches products

**Listen for** — Whether shared values live once or are retyped per product, which is `MODEL-05` and the most common structural weakness there is. Whether the same concept has two coding systems — an internal one and a standard one — and whether anything maps between them. Whether things that are not products but belong to them have a home at all.

**Note** — Change a brand name and ask how many places would need editing. The number is the finding.

#### `ASK-52` — Across the business

> **“Do all parts of the business describe products the same way? What happens when they do not?”**

**Covers** `MODEL-01` `MODEL-03` `MODEL-08`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns product information · somebody from outside the product team

**Listen for** — Whether one structure serves everyone or each unit has its own. Whether that was decided or inherited through acquisition. What the divergence actually costs — sometimes nothing, and saying so is more useful than treating every difference as a defect.

**Note** — Pairs with `ASK-16`, which asks the same thing about ways of working. Structure and habit diverge separately and for different reasons.

#### `ASK-53` — Codes, and history

> **“Where do your product codes come from — could you run out? And can you see what a product looked like a year ago?”**

**Covers** `MODEL-09` `MODEL-10` `MODEL-19`

**Who to invite** — whoever owns the integrations · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Who issues identifiers and whether the space has a ceiling. Running out sounds absurd until it happens, and organisations on legacy numbering schemes are often closer than they think. On history: whether old versions exist, and whether anyone has ever needed one — usually for a dispute or a regulator.

**Note** — `MODEL-19` cleared at a second client by measurement, not testimony: an article number 93% through its digit width while four sessions never mentioned it. Keep asking, but expect silence — running out is discovered, not felt. The honest check is the identifier's format and the range in use, from an export.

#### `ASK-54` — Built to order

> **“Does anything you sell get configured or built to a customer's specification? How is that described?”**

**Covers** `MODEL-06` `MODEL-11` `MODEL-20`

**Who to invite** — a merchandiser · whoever owns product information

**Listen for** — Whether allowed combinations are defined anywhere or live in a salesperson's head. Whether each configuration becomes a record or is assembled at the point of sale. Whether individual physical units are ever described separately — `MODEL-20`, also provisional, and the second-hand and refurbished trade is where it shows up.

**Note** — If the answer is no, say so and move on. `+configurator` is the model's clearest single-ability capability and a substantial purchase for a narrow need.

### Using them

Nobody runs 54 questions. A workshop uses eight or ten, chosen by which domains are live for this client —
and `ASK-01`, `ASK-12`, `ASK-32` and `ASK-48` between them reach 24 abilities across nine domains, which is a
serviceable first session on their own.

Two habits worth keeping. **Ask for an instance, not an assessment** — the rule every one of these is written
to. And **let the diagnostics fire on their own**: the four DAM triggers are spread across four questions
that never mention asset management, and the `+mdm` pair sits inside a question about duplicates. A client
who arrives at the need themselves owns it; a client told about it defends against it.
